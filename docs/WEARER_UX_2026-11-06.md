# Wearer experience redesign (Nov 6)

**Primary design spec for Run 8 onward.** It follows a walk-through of the Run 7 build and captures from the point of view of someone wearing Spectacles, and Wadood's decisions on Nov 6. It builds on [the judges' review notes](REVIEW_SESSION_2026-11-05.md).

## The problem in one line

The engine and ideas are strong, but the experience is still built like a screen. Panels, buttons and text are stacked over the suspects inside a small field of view.

## Audit of the Run 7 build (from the captures)

- **Opening:** zone names float on the wall at arbitrary spots, with a row of yellow brackets along the bottom edge. LEAF test banners leak into some captures.
- **Casting sweep:** labels on the real furniture. **The strongest AR moment so far.** Fix role plausibility: a speaker is not a believable weapon.
- **Questioning:**
  - Vale stands **on the coffee table, inside the crate**, and fills the view.
  - A **huge** four-button text panel covers her side. The subtitle crosses her chest. The crate's Notes/Evidence/Accuse buttons are hidden behind her.
- **Memory card:** floats across Vale's torso, and a cyan toast is cut off at the edge.
- **Props:** primitive shapes (a yellow tower, a flat clock disc, a blue box), and the crate is a plain box.
- **Ending:** an oversized "CASE CLOSED" overflowing its frame, a "Details" bar, and leftover pawns.

## Principles for the wearer

1. **A small window.** Spectacles draw graphics in roughly 46° diagonal.
   - A life-size suspect at about 1.8 m is taller than that window. Conversation framing is head and shoulders at 1.3–1.5 m; full figures are at 2.5–3 m.
   - Keep **at most one UI element in any one direction of view**, and never put UI over a face.
2. **Additive display: slight adjustments only** (Wadood).
   - Prefer bright line UI and thin frames over heavy dark plates.
   - Treat the blackout as the virtual world going dark plus bright lightning, since the glasses can't darken the real room.
   - Don't rebuild the look for this; small tweaks only.
3. **Few words.** Voices carry dialogue and the room carries clues; text is short labels.
   - **Subtitles are ON by default:** one short line below the speaker's face, with word reveal.
4. **Hands and neck:**
   - a few medium-sized pinch targets, not giant word slabs and not tiny buttons;
   - short pinch-and-drag gestures;
   - interactions at waist height within arm's reach;
   - minimal repeated looking down.
5. **Spatial sound is half of AR.** Voices come from where each suspect stands, thunder from the window, a whisper from behind.

## Decisions (Wadood, Nov 6)

- **Redesign every menu as interactive objects (all of it).** That includes the opening mode choice and the "place the case" step.
- **Wow moments:** build 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 below. **Not 2** (suspects sitting on furniture or hidden behind it); no spike.
- **Subtitles on.** Design for the glasses with slight adjustments only.
- **Multiplayer is dropped** from this submission; see later.md.

## Object-based UI (replaces the menus)

1. **Opening: two case files on the crate.**
   - A sealed brown folder stamped **"THE HARLOW TRUST"** is the Preset.
   - A blank folder with a magnifying glass, **"CAST THIS ROOM"**, is Dynamic.
   - Pinch one, lift it, and pull it open to start that mode. No buttons.
2. **Placing the case: interactive.**
   - The crate arrives as a glowing outline you **carry and set down** on a real surface. Detected table and floor surfaces show a landing ring, and it locks with a thud and a dust puff.
   - Director/film mode still auto-places, so the Preview demo isn't slowed down.
3. **Asking by presenting evidence** (the L.A. Noire move, made physical):
   - **Hold a memory photograph up to a suspect,** or draw a thread from an object to them, to ask about that object.
   - **Point at another guest while facing a suspect** to ask about that person.
   - Beside the suspect's shoulder, about **three small glyph tokens** (icon plus one word): name, whereabouts, and "you're lying" (press). A small "more" glyph pages them. No text panel.
4. **Palm notebook.** Turn your **left palm up** and the evidence fan, notebook and accuse controls appear on your hand. Turn it down and they're gone.
   - Use the Spectacles Interaction Kit's hand-menu pattern where it's available.
   - Keep a small Preview fallback (one glyph on the crate, or a key).
5. **The case board on your real wall.**
   - The Clue grid and evidence board are pinned to a real wall: one detected by the room scan in Dynamic, an authored Evening Room wall in Preset.
   - Memory photos pin themselves there, red string connects them, and ruled-out suspects, rooms and weapons get crosses.
   - You walk over to read it. The notebook panel becomes optional.
6. **Accusing:**
   - **point at a suspect,** and a spotlight beam runs from your finger to them;
   - choose room and weapon by pointing or dragging their pawns on the crate;
   - **press your thumb into the wax seal** (hold pinch) to commit.
7. **Feedback near the action, not at screen edges** (edges fall outside the window on glasses):
   - "Deduction filed" appears as a stamp landing on the wall board;
   - "Credibility −1" is a crack in the lens icon on the palm notebook;
   - a new Hunch is a small glow on the relevant object.
8. **Props in the actors' glowing line style:** the clock, recorder, candlestick, letter knife, bookend, mug and the crate itself, as engraved-light line drawings (GLB with a line or unlit shader, or billboards). No flat-coloured primitives. **Casting assigns plausible roles** (cables, lamps and heavy decor as weapons; clocks and lamps as witnesses; sofas and cabinets as hiding places).
9. **Zone names as chalk circles on the floor** at their furniture clusters, not words on walls.
10. **Ending:**
    - a small CASE CLOSED stamp lands on the wall board, and you pinch it for details;
    - **the Morning Edition newspaper appears in your hands** (wow 8);
    - leftover pawns and debug overlays are hidden.
11. **Housekeeping:**
    - suspects stand on free floor, never on furniture or the crate;
    - the conversation spot is 1.3–1.5 m, with full figures across the room;
    - LEAF banners and diagnostics are hidden in Director/film mode;
    - no UI over faces.

## Wow moments (approved)

1. **The room becomes the crime scene:**
   - the casting sweep on real furniture;
   - **crime-scene tape across the real doorway** ("Nobody leaves"; Pike's escape attempt happens there);
   - **a chalk outline on the real floor** where Harlow fell;
   - **rain and lightning on the real window**, with spatial thunder. Lightning briefly reveals a ghost silhouette.
2. ~~Suspects sitting on furniture or occluded by it~~: **not doing this**.
3. **Objects replay what they saw.** A pulled memory blooms into a 5-second, real-scale ghost vignette at the object. At the kettle at 22:30: nobody there, no steam, a cold kettle.
4. **The evidence web.** Every objection and deduction strings red thread across the room between people, objects and places. At the end the whole web lights up and converges on Vale, ending on the wall board.
5. **Walk around the frozen murder.** The reconstruction freezes at 22:30: the mug suspended above the coffee table, the cable in Vale's hands. On glasses you walk around it; in Preview a scripted camera orbit gives the video shot.
6. **A UV flashlight in your hand.** During the dark beat your palm emits a violet cone, and you sweep the real floor to reveal the drag marks.
7. **Spatial sound cues:**
   - Wren whispers from behind you ("over here");
   - a knock comes from your real door;
   - each suspect's voice comes from where they stand.
8. **The newspaper in your hands** at the ending, with the headline built from the case log.
9. **Dusting for fingerprints.** Wave your hand over a real surface to reveal glowing prints. Use it for one or two evidence items (the mug, for example) as a short, playful alternative to pinching.

All of these stay true to the engine: replays, strings, ghosts and headlines show only engine facts and admitted statements.

## Visual acceptance checklist (use on every capture)

1. Nothing over a speaker's face. At most one UI element in each direction of view.
2. Subtitles are one short line below the face, and readable.
3. Targets are pinchable (about 2–3° tall), with no giant text slabs and no tiny buttons.
4. No debug or LEAF overlays in Director/film captures.
5. Props are in line style; no primitive boxes.
6. Things sit on real surfaces: tape on the doorway, chalk on the floor, the board on a wall, rain on the window.
7. The palette is coral, cyan, ivory and brass, with bright lines over heavy dark plates.
8. The scene reads in under 2 seconds: what to do next is obvious from the objects themselves.

## Verdict

The engine, the rules and the evidence of agentic building are strong. Every earlier engine fix holds, the git history contains no secrets, and there are 49 package-sized commits plus 21 LEAF scenarios. **What a judge would see today undersells all of it:**

1. **Silent suspects.** The saved scene has `stageMode: true` (`Scene.scene:854`), and Stage mode drops all audio (`RoomscriptTts.ts:32,54`). When suspects do speak, the strict validator forces near-verbatim canonical lines ("My lighter is empty."). Live audio arrived in only 18 of 28 calls.
2. **It looks like a prototype:**
   - six or seven overlapping grey UIKit cards;
   - placeholder boxes over the photoreal Evening Room;
   - suspects drawn as dark ink on ivory, the opposite of design 03;
   - three suspects with one image for every pose;
   - an OBJECTION in the default font;
   - the best beats drawn as 1–2 cm strips.
3. **The story's big moments never appear in Lens.** The letter, voicemail, Pike's flight and the blackout/UV beat fire only in the engine. The reconstruction shows 12 s of an empty room, then 10 s of a static repeat.
4. **Interrogation doesn't pay off:**
   - An all-talk route crosses out nothing on the grid.
   - Pinching 11 objects scores 3/3.
   - Hunches do nothing (F11).
   - The lamp spoils the tea alibi, and lamp plus cable solves the case in 20 minutes.
5. **Dynamism is invisible.** The case compiler exists only in the engine, and generated cases crash `SetupCase` (N6).
6. **The integrated playthrough has never passed in Preview.** The Oct 30 timeouts look like the Lens Studio process slowly degrading over long, reload-heavy sessions (run handshake 0.2 s → 88 s within a process; 357–543 hot reloads), made worse by our own per-frame deep copies.

None of this needs a redesign. All of it is polish and wiring on top of a sound engine.

**Wadood's direction (Nov 3):** the overall idea works. The biggest remaining gaps are:
1. **polish in UI design and character design**;
2. **a case that's more straightforward about what exactly happened**;
3. **music and voices.**

These outrank everything else in Groups 1–4.

## Art direction (D2)

The prototype pipeline works: Three.js in headless Chromium produces deterministic PNG, GLB and MP4 output from one command. See [`reviews/2026-11-03-media/asset-contact-sheet.jpg`](reviews/2026-11-03-media/asset-contact-sheet.jpg). **But the first-pass portraits shouldn't ship as they are:**
- the four suspects share one body, one suit and near-identical friendly faces;
- they read as generic vector avatars rather than 1940s noir;
- the silhouettes don't differ, so a judge can't tell who's who at a glance.

The reconstruction mockups ([`recon-C.jpg`](reviews/2026-11-03-media/recon-C.jpg)) have a much stronger look: **luminous line art on transparency**, coral for truth, cyan for claims, ivory for the innocent, and glowing particles. That style also suits the additive display, and it unifies actors, ghosts and VFX. **Art direction v2:**

- **One style everywhere: bright engraving/neon line work, no fills.** Hatching for tone. The tell prop is the brightest pixel. A soft fade below the waist (paper theatre).
- **A distinct silhouette per suspect**, readable at thumbnail size:
  - **Vale:** a 1940s dress with a fur stole, the brooch, an upswept hairstyle, upright posture.
  - **Imran Qadir:** a loose suit, an open collar and the lighter; slouched charm.
  - **Dr. Ada Okafor:** a doctor's coat over a dress, glasses, a bag; still and composed.
  - **Pike:** greatcoat, glove, a hunched, edgy stance.
  - **Harlow:** victim and ghost only.
- **Expressive poses.** Five per suspect: neutral, listening, rattled, broken and smug or guarded. Each should be clearly different at a glance: posture, hands, head angle and face. Use stronger noir faces, not cartoon eyes.
- **Supporting art:**
  - type: a display face for hero words (OBJECTION, CASE CLOSED) and a typewriter face for cards, both OFL;
  - cards: Polaroid evidence cards generated from the case data;
  - props: GLB with 1940s detail;
  - scenes: blackout/UV and reconstruction sprites.
- **Self-check:** the generator renders a contact sheet. The agent reviews it against this list before wiring anything, and saves before/after captures.

### Group 1: game and story (engine and content, mostly Node-testable)

- **G2. Hero case v3** (gameplay WP3).

  - Fix the text: "was at the desk", plus confession wording.
  - **Tests:** a talk-only route reaches one hypothesis within 180 minutes; every lie is breakable in every order; par is recomputed.
- **G3. Story rewrites.** Apply the about 20 rewritten lines and five story moves in the gameplay report §3, keeping Vale / Study / cable intact.
  - Surface the hidden gem at the reveal: "every small lie told you where they stood".
- **G4. Case clarity: what exactly happened** (Wadood's priority).
  - **One plain story spine,** written once in `Content/STORY.md` and tested against the facts:

    > Harlow found Vale's forged trust accounts and planned to expose her. At 22:30 in the Study she strangled him with the charger cable; the mug fell from the desk. She arranged him so it would look like his heart, and claimed she was in the Kitchen making tea, but the kettle was cold all night. Every other guest lied only to hide a smaller secret.

    Correct it to match the real hero facts if any detail differs.
  - **Opening, "the question":** a case-file card, voiced by the narrator. It names the victim, where and when he was found, the four guests and one question: who, where, with what.
  - **During play:** a **"What we know" timeline strip** on the crate lid, built only from `PlayerKnowledge`. It shows certified times and places as the player proves them, plus each suspect's current story, with a tick or cross.
  - **Lies read as lies.** When a lie breaks, the slip shows "SAID: Kitchen, 22:30" crossed out and "PROVEN: Study, 22:30" written under it, so the player always knows what changed.
  - **Ending:** the reconstruction narrates the spine in 4–6 short, voiced sentences, each citing a fact. "What they were hiding" gives one line per innocent: why they lied and what was really going on.
  - **Fewer moving parts:**
    - remove or merge facts and evidence that don't serve the spine or a suspect's secret;
    - keep at most one clue per deduction on the main path;
    - make the motive explicit at the voicemail beat.
  - **Acceptance:**
    - a pure test checks every sentence of the spine against facts;
    - the opening card, timeline strip and ending narration show in LEAF captures;
    - in the terminal, a naive route ends with a summary a newcomer can retell in one breath.

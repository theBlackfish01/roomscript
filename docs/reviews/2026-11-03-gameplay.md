## 1. Verdict

**The bones are good.**
- The engine is honest.
- The lie ladder for Vale (tea → "already dead" → "the cable was in my hands") is a real ladder.
- The baseline "harmless lie" tells teach the system cleanly.
- N1, N2 and N5 landed: a guess can't get 3 stars, timeout allows one accusation, and arguments need exact sets.

**But today a first-time player meets three problems:**
1. **Interrogation still isn't load-bearing.**
   - A pure talker spends 165 of 180 minutes and crosses out **nothing** on the grid (40 hypotheses left).
   - An object-pincher who asks one 5-minute question gets **3/3**.
   - The player who plays the designed loop and breaks all five lies gets **2/3**, and holds 6 useless Hunches.
   - N3 landed only half.
2. **The suspects don't sound like people.** Stage mode (the saved demo setup) speaks the engine's own claim text:
   - "My lighter is empty."
   - "That is outside my account."
   - "I brought a forged certificate."

   The validator's 70% content-word rule (`LineValidator.ts:102`) blocks any flavour, live or authored. This is the single biggest story and design gap, and it costs the design and presentation scores.
3. **Most of the drama is invisible in Lens.**
   - The four dealt beats (letter, voicemail, Pike's flight and the blackout/UV twist) fire in the engine, but no Lens script reacts to a `beat` event. They land silently in the wallet.
   - The ending shows no confession, no secrets and no payoff. It is a 24-second slot loop ("The room at 21:00…"), then "Holmes rating 2 / 3", the case ID and a hash.

**Dynamism.** The case compiler exists in the engine only. A player or judge sees one fixed case, with authored lines, and none of it is visibly "AI". Generated cases aren't demo-quality: third-person self-claims, 7–11 "case recorder" props, 20–40-minute solutions, and every one crashes `refuterMarks`.

**All three are fixable in one or two Codex passes.** Most of the work is content and pure-engine changes, which are cheap for Wadood to test. The Preview responsiveness blocker (PROGRESS: JS timeouts) still gates every Lens item. It should be cleared first.

### What works
- **The lies are breakable by reasoning, and they read well:**
  - lighter vs the door's flame;
  - glasses vs the kettle's reflection;
  - gloves vs the dripping window;
  - tea vs the cold kettle;
  - "already dead" vs the recorder.
- The gaze line always points at the object that breaks the lie, so the loop is learnable within 2 minutes.
- **A hidden gem:** every harmless baseline lie secretly encodes where that suspect really stood. Imran's lighter is seen by the Hall door, Okafor's glasses in the Kitchen kettle, Pike's gloves at the Conservatory window, Vale's brooch by the Study lamp. Nothing tells the player this. It's the best "aha" in the case (see §3).
- Timeout now lets you accuse (N2 landed). A 30-minute blind guess can't earn more than 1 star (N1 landed). Dumping evidence into arguments is rejected (N5 landed).
- **Pacing.** The tutorial route is about 31 actions, so about 12–15 minutes in Preview at roughly 25 s per action. It fits the 10–15 minute session. The 3-minute video needs only about 8 of those actions.

### What's wrong, ranked

**G1. Interrogation pays nothing on the grid.**
- The victim's location comes only from the lamp or the mug.
- So the three witness alibis ("I saw Imran in the Hall") and four weapon sightings cross out nothing until an object is pinched.
- Okafor's "I heard Harlow answer Vale after she entered the Study" obviously places Harlow in the Study, but the engine doesn't count it.

**G2. The scoring still rewards skipping the fun.**
- The efficiency star compares time against an 85-minute par that needs no broken lie.
- Hunches earned by correct objections do nothing (F11 is still open): `chooseHunch` only re-points at an already-noted cover, and after the ladders are broken it returns `no_observed_cover`.

**G3. The lamp spoils the tutorial.**
- Pinching the lamp to break the brooch lie also prints "Vale beside the Study desk at 22:30" (`E_VALE_LAMP`).
- So Vale's tea alibi is dead before she even gives it, and the kettle objection, the best beat, loses its tension.

**G4. The cable is a confession.** "The cable remembers Vale's hands. Harlow's collar fibres…" Lamp plus cable solves the murder in 20 minutes; the rest is bookkeeping. No innocent ever looks guilty.
- Okafor (a missing medicine bottle) and Pike (a forgery Harlow could expose) have real red-herring potential.
- But they only ever surface at the moment they're cleared.

**G5. Zero pips means instant loss with no accusation** (route C). It's the same dead end N2 fixed for time. The "OVERRULED: does_not_refute" reason never says *why* in human terms.

**G6. Press is a dead verb for innocents.** It repeats the same sentence for 15 minutes. Only Vale's tea alibi has a pressed variant.

**G7. The beats are clock-driven and arbitrary.**
- The blackout fires at minute 120, often in the middle of the weapon-pinching chores.
- The voicemail breaks Vale's second account for free at minute 75.
- That's fine for fairness, but they need a stage moment (see G-list item 2).

**G8. Text nits.**
- "Harlow **stood** in the Study" contradicts "moved from the Study **chair**".
- The confession says "my forged trust".
- `play.ts` chips print raw IDs ("Tell me about O3"); the Lens labels them correctly.

## 3. Story

**The mystery is coherent and fair, but flat.** The parts that work:
- a trustee who forged the books;
- the tea alibi;
- "he was already dead when I got there";
- "the cable was in my hands".

**What's missing is any doubt, voice and payoff.**

### Five concrete writing moves, all keeping the envelope (Vale / Study / cable) and every fact intact

**1. Pay off the baseline lies at the reveal (an epilogue card plus a Wren line).**
> "Every small lie tonight told you where they really stood. Imran's lighter flared by the Hall door. Okafor's glasses fogged over the Kitchen kettle. Pike's gloves dripped at the Conservatory window. And Mrs. Vale's brooch came open in the Study, where the cable caught it."
- This needs one new true fact, revealed only after the verdict: the clasp tore on the cable.
- Text only, at the reveal. The baseline rules don't change.

**2. Give Vale a noir motive with a sting.**
> "Edmund's own speculations emptied the Harlow Trust. For twenty years I forged the ledgers to hide *his* ruin. Tomorrow he meant to hand them to the police and call me the thief."
- This doesn't change `F_VALE_MOTIVE` (forgery exposure); it only colours the confession.

**3. Explain the moved body, and tie Okafor in.**
> "She lowered him to the floor so it would look like his heart. There was a doctor in the house to sign for it."
- Use it as the reconstruction caption at 22:41.
- It turns the UV twist from trivia ("moved from chair to floor") into intent.

**4. Seed one real red herring** (new true facts, solver-checked as harmless).
- The medicine bottle remembers "a dose missing, a doctor's hands at 22:15".
- Players suspect poison and Okafor until the kettle, the window and the witnesses clear her.
- The bottle still "remained in the Kitchen at 22:30", so the weapon logic is unchanged.

**5. Lean into the room joke.** The opening caption or Wren:
> "Your charger cable, cast tonight as THE ROPE. 1947 didn't have USB-C. Harlow wasn't fussy."

Judges smile at the room being in on it.

## 5. Dynamism: what exists versus what's seen

**In code:**
- `CaseCompiler`, `Simulator`, `Dealer`, `StatementBuilder`, `BeatPlanner` and `RepairPlanner`;
- 6 authored pitches;
- 5 room fixtures, including `wadood-room`;
- 200 out of 200 cases compile in tests, at under 300 ms each.

The live LLM pitch path is engine-only, and its acceptance is unmeasured.

**What a player sees:** one fixed hero case, with authored captions in Stage mode. None of it reads as AI.

**Generated-case quality, measured over 50 cases:**
- `refuterMarks` throws in 50/50 (N6 is still open, because it hard-codes `where@`), so the Lens bridge would crash on load.
- The minimum route is 20–40 minutes against a 180-minute clock, so they're trivially short.
- They need 7–11 virtual props, mostly "Study case recorder 2"-style recorders.
- Self-claims are in the third person: Esther Wren says "Esther Wren was in Lounge at 23:00." Covers read "I did not borrow the item." / "I was attending to my own business."
- Only the culprit lies about location, so innocents have no location tells.
- `D_ALIBI` uses the same proof as opportunity; no heard alibi is required.
- Names collide: "Simon Vale" and "Esther Wren" (the partner's name).

**The smallest high-impact way to *show* dynamism:**
1. **A "Case Compiler" page on the crate lid (WP6a).**
   - "Deal a new case" compiles three pitches against the *current Preview room* in front of the viewer, in under 1 s.
   - Each shows a card: title, victim, four suspects with roles and secrets, and "your kettle → THE KITCHEN, your charger → THE ROPE".
   - Solver ticks: ✓ one solution, ✓ every lie breakable, ✓ two routes, plus the sealed hash.
   - That's 10–15 honest seconds of video: "every case is proven before you play it". It needs the N6 fix and the StatementBuilder cleanup.
2. **"Case B: The Last Broadcast", playable (WP6b, stretch).**
   - Compile one library pitch on the hero fixture.
   - Author its line bank offline (Claude in Tooling, as was done for the hero), and freeze it like the hero.
   - Honest claim: "truth compiled by the engine, voices written by AI".
   - Selectable from the crate.
3. **One live line in the video.** A typed oddball question answered live in character, with the validator badge.
4. **Devpost wording.** Say "the hero case is authored and engine-verified; the compiler generates and proves new cases from your room's objects". Don't claim live room-to-case play unless WP6b ships.

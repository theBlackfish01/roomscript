# Roomscript engine and rules review, second pass (2026-10-29)

**Scope:** deterministic engine and game rules. **Repo:** `RoomScriptF` at `5923208`, plus the uncommitted C7/D1 working tree. Read-only: nothing in the repo was changed.

**Repro scripts** (run with `Tooling\node_modules\.bin\tsx`, importing the engine by `file:///` URL), all in `<local-development-path>`:
- `lib.mts`
- `p1_fixes.mts`: E1–E4 and A4
- `p2_new.mts`: C6, C7, D1 and the P2 solver items
- `p3_routes.mts`: routes and scoring
- `p4_gen.mts`: generated cases

**Targeted tests:** `npx vitest run tests/game tests/content tests/player tests/case/Solver.test.ts` → **9 files, 97/97 passed** (2.6 s). This includes the new `spatial-objection` and `clue-presentation` tests. The terminal demo (`npx tsx scripts/play.ts --demo`) wins at 175/180 minutes.

## Verdict

- **The previous P0 fixes are real.** E1, E2, E3 and A4 hold against every new exploit I tried. E4 removed the zero-minute pinch-all. Each fix has a regression test.
- **The new C6, C7 and D1 engine code keeps the truth rule.**
  - Tells come only from engine events.
  - An object drop never guesses which memory to use.
  - Proven marks use only true facts.
  - The notebook and grid use player knowledge only.
  - Private cards need an observed fact.
- **The scoring is still broken in a new way** (new P0). A 30-minute informed guess, with 20 hypotheses still open, earns the top 3/3 Holmes rating. The only unrefuted suggestion is always the envelope, so a "breathes in" confirms that guess for 15 more minutes.
- **The game-feel problems are now the bigger risk:**
  - Interrogation is never needed, and it is the slowest way to learn anything.
  - The 180-minute clock is too tight for anyone who plays the interrogation as designed.
  - Timeout is a hard loss with no accusation, even when the final pinch completes the grid.
- **D1 has a replay determinism gap:** the setup-pose refuter order is not captured by `caseId` or the action log.
- **None of the P2 solver items (F4–F8, F12–F15) were touched.**

## New findings

### P0

**N1. Holmes 3/3 rewards a guess, and "breathes in" is a perfect oracle** (`Engine/game/Scoring.ts:7-12`)
- **Repro (`p3_routes.mts`, `p1_fixes.mts`):**
  - Pinch the lamp, the cable and the bed cover (30 minutes, 20 hypotheses still possible), file the four deductions, then accuse: **rating 3, 30 minutes**.
  - Pinch the lamp and the cable, `Suggest S2 Z1 O1` → `breathesIn`, pinch the bed cover, then accuse: **3/3 at 45 minutes**, with zero questions.
- **Why:**
  - Efficiency is measured as elapsed time against the *uniqueness* minimum (80). The score never checks that the player's knowledge actually pinned down the answer.
  - The required deductions are all about Vale, so three pinches support all of them.
  - In the hero, and in 5/5 generated cases (`p4_gen.mts`), exactly one tuple is unrefuted at the start: the envelope. Refuter availability only grows, so a "breathes in" always means the envelope. The decision that "an unrefuted suggestion is not a verdict" is true in principle but never in practice.
- **Fix:**
  - Award the 2nd and 3rd stars only when `solvePlayerKnowledge(graph, state.knowledge).hypotheses.length === 1` at the moment of accusation, or count a breathes-in as knowledge and include Suggest in the minimum-route search.
  - Benchmark efficiency against the cheapest *supported* route: uniqueness plus every deduction, which is 90 for the hero.
  - Add a test that a 30-minute guess scores at most 1.

### P1

**N2. Timeout is a dead end, even when it is caused by the final clue** (`Clock.ts:6`, `Reducer.ts:77`)
- **Repro (`p3_routes.mts`, the "tutorial" route):** baseline lesson, Vale's ladder, other alibis, then weapons. The last weapon pinch reaches 180 minutes, the grid becomes unique (1 hypothesis), and `verdict` is already `timeout`. `Accuse` is then rejected with `case_closed`.
- A "natural" player who asks each suspect's name and alibi spends 120 minutes on 8 questions and times out with 4 hypotheses left.
- **Fix:** at timeout, close investigation verbs but allow one accusation (the design says "suspects leave at midnight", not "you lose"). Alternatively, apply timeout only when `elapsed > clock`.

**N3. Interrogation is optional and the slowest way to learn anything** (design and economy)
- Ask costs 15 minutes. Pinch costs 10 and often returns 2–3 memories. Objects never lie.
- The test-enshrined 3/3 route (`scoring.test.ts:30-40`) asks **zero** questions.
- The best route that uses Vale's ladder is 95 minutes. The design's tutorial path (brooch baseline, Vale's ladder, other alibis) takes 175–180 minutes, scores 2/3 at best, and times out if it strays.
- `D_ALIBI` ("Vale's tea alibi is false") is credited from `E_VALE_LAMP` even when the alibi was never heard: notebook empty, `argumentSupported` (`HeroCaseHarlow1041.ts:168`).
- **Fix (pick a small set):**
  - Make `D_ALIBI` routes require the observed cover, e.g. `[["C_VALE_ALIBI_0","E_KETTLE_COLD"],["C_VALE_ALIBI_0","E_VALE_LAMP"]]`.
  - Make `baseline:name` questions free and Ask 10 minutes.
  - Raise the clock to about 240.
  - Have the solver report "routes requiring a statement" as a design metric.

**N4. The D1 refuter order is outside the replay identity** (`RoomscriptEngineBridge.ts:18`, `CluePresentation.ts:17`)
- The bridge overwrites `graph.refutation.order` from the setup pose but keeps `caseId`.
- Facing −z versus +z changes the revealed card in **77/100** suggestions. A log replayed against the other graph diverges silently (`p2_new.mts`): `caseId` is equal and knowledge differs.
- The decision log says "retain in the case graph for deterministic replay", but nothing outside the Lens process retains it.
- **Fix:** log the order as a seq-1 `Setup{refutationOrder}` action (or setup pose) that the reducer stores in `GameState`, or fold the order into `caseId`. Add a replay test against a freshly created graph.

**N5. Argument claims accept supersets (the E1 exploit, but for deductions)** (`Reducer.ts:174`, `Scoring.ts:8`)
- Filing every deduction with *all* known refs credits all four (`p3_routes.mts`, oracle route).
- This doesn't change truth, but it makes the closing argument and the "supported" star unable to tell reasoning from dumping. It will matter for E7 and LLM extraction.
- **Fix:** require the cited refs to equal one support route (or allow at most one extra ref), as E1 does.

### P2

- **N6. `refuterMarks` hard-codes `where@` topics** (`CluePresentation.ts:7`). It throws `Missing claimed mark for S1` on 5/5 generated cases, whose topic is `whereabouts` (`p4_gen.mts`). This is harmless while only the hero ships, but the bridge would crash at init on any generated case. **Fix:** find the layer-0 statement asserting an `at` fact for the speaker at `murderSlot`, regardless of topic.
- **N7. The absence and composure rules are loose:**
  - A lawyered suspect still refutes in the suggestion sweep (`RefutationRound.ts:8-11`): S2 shows the bookend card while absent.
  - Free `RevealEvidence` of irrelevant items resets empty presses (`Reducer.ts:140`): 8 presses on Imran, never lawyered (`p1_fixes` E2b).
  - Composure reaches 0 with no engine consequence. Pike sits at 0/3 for several turns, while `RoomscriptSuspectActor.ts:37` shows a "broken" pose for a suspect who may still be lying.
  - A Press while absent reports `no_current_claim` instead of `lawyered`.
  - **Fix:** skip absent holders in `firstRefuter` (or document the exception); reset empty presses only for evidence relevant to the claim (a breaker or `about` holder); either link composure to lawyering or stop the pose reading it.
- **N8. Tells and gaze point straight at the breaker.** Every cover's gaze `about` object is the holder of its breaker (`p2_new.mts` table: kettle, recorder, door, window, lamp). With 100% tells and "TELL NOTED" in the notebook, the only deduction left is choosing among that object's 1–3 memories. This matches design 01 and 03, so it is not a bug. Expect the hero to feel guided; the design's "Noir removes the gaze line" difficulty is the release valve.
- **N9. The visual sweep may disagree with the engine order** (*suspected*, Lens side). The bridge computes the order from the `player` input, while `RoomscriptClueController.ts:31-33` recomputes angles from `flow.actor.camera` at OnStart. If these differ, the blade can visually pass a holder that the engine would have reached first. Suspect marks also stay at the claimed setup zones after C7 walks a suspect to the proven mark. A setup gaze straight down (forward x,z ≈ 0) gives an unstable order. **Fix:** have the controller read angles from the stored order and pose, and fall back to the yaw when the projected forward is near zero.
- **N10. Stale text:**
  - `play.ts:24` still says "inspection is free".
  - `scoreCase` re-runs `minimumCostRoute` on every call (about 15 ms). Cache it per graph.
- **Carried over:** F4–F8, F12, F13, F14 and F15 as listed in the table. Do F4 and F6 first: E3 showed that hand-listed breakers go stale, and nothing checks them.

## Determinism and truth-rule check of the new code

**TellCue** (`TellCue.ts`)
- Fires only for the latest gameplay action's `tell` event, with the active cover statement.
- Stays quiet for deflection, truthful, lawyered, filler and confession lines.
- An audit-only `LineSpoken` doesn't invalidate the cue, which matches the decision.

**SpatialObjection** (`SpatialObjection.ts`)
- Returns a memory only when exactly one is observed. Latent-but-unfired memories are never offered; zone-held items (letter, UV) are never droppable.
- `provenMark` moves only to true `at` facts, and baseline corrections don't move anyone.
- The controller sends nothing when the drop is empty, so no pip is lost.

**CluePresentation** (`CluePresentation.ts`)
- Order and angle are pure and stable on ties.
- The private card requires `privateTo` and a learned fact.
- Grid crosses come only from `solvePlayerKnowledge`.

No `Math.random`, `Date.now` or `localeCompare` appears in the new files.

**Decisions vs code:** the E1–E4, A4, C6, C7 and D1 entries match the code. The exceptions are N4 (the replay claim) and N1 ("not a verdict" is false for every case tested).

## Game feel (scripted fresh-player runs)

**Lies are breakable by reasoning, and they read well:**
- lighter "empty" vs the door's flame;
- glasses vs the kettle reflection;
- gloves vs dripping window water;
- the tea alibi vs the cold kettle;
- "already dead" vs the recording.

**Dead ends hurt:**
- Wrong objections cost a pip, and 0 pips ends the game.
- Timeout ends it with no accusation (N2).
- The clock spends 15 minutes on "What is your name?"

**Overall:**
- The case is solved *as a human* by the lamp plus the cable ("Vale's hands. Harlow's collar fibres") after 20 minutes. The open-world solver doesn't model that inference.
- Everything after that is bookkeeping: four weapon pinches and three alibi pinches.
- The fun parts (tells, objections, Vale's ladder) are optional detours that cost the 3rd star.
- Hunches pile up unused.

**Fastest wins:** N1, N2 and the `D_ALIBI` change in N3. Then consider making one innocent alibi or one weapon location provable only by testimony, so the interrogation is load-bearing.

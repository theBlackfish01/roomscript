# Find, ask, catch: the Run 10 redesign (Nov 7)

**Status:** Wadood's decision, Sat Nov 7 night, after his own playthrough of the Run 9 build. From Run 10 onward this is **the primary design spec for flow, interaction and audio**.
- **Still applies:** [`ART_2D_2026-11-07.md`](ART_2D_2026-11-07.md) for art. From [`WEARER_UX_2026-11-06.md`](WEARER_UX_2026-11-06.md), its "Principles for the wearer" and its "Visual acceptance checklist".
- **Superseded:** WEARER_UX's object-based UI and its wow-moment list, wherever this spec says otherwise.
- **Speech recognition is out.** Wadood, Nov 7: "Skip the speech recognition." No ASR work this run. The typed free question stays (see "Ask").

## What Wadood found in his playthrough (organized account, Nov 7)
- A female voice sometimes plays underneath the main narration.
- Clicking and moving things is hard. That process needs to be much simpler.
- The items don't play as much of a part as they could, and they don't make much sense.
- Parts are overcomplicated. Focus on what works: talking to people in their voices.
- The narration over-explains ("the sofa is the witness"), and that sounds silly.
- Parts of the experience have lost coherence.
- The art and the pictures look good.
- Some of the menu UI is unnecessary in the way it's shown. Walking up to people and pinching them makes sense.
- Voices sometimes cut off halfway through a line, and the action buttons pop up inconsistently.

## Causes found in the code (Nov 7 review)
1. **The female voice is Wren.**
   - `RoomscriptWrenHints` plays through `RoomscriptAudio.play` / `playAt`, the sound-effect pool (`RoomscriptMain.ts:70`). Nothing there waits for the narrator or a suspect.
   - W5 is scheduled 25 s after the investigation starts with `needed: () => true`, so it always plays.
2. **Lines get cut off.**
   - `RoomscriptInterrogationController.cancel()` calls `tts.stop()`, and every `ask()` and `selectSuspect()` calls `cancel()`. So pointing at another guest, or presenting to another suspect, cuts the current speaker.
   - `RoomscriptTts.speak()` starts with `this.stop()`. The filler line (spoken without `await`) is therefore cut off by the real answer.
   - The Preview audio memory growth (audiodg) can add clipping as well.
3. **The buttons jump.**
   - `RoomscriptCastController.proximity()` uses a hard 150 cm range and `dot > .55` (about 57°) with no hysteresis.
   - `focus()` shows the question chips and hides the verb bar, and the verb bar appears at other moments. So two button sets take turns in the same spot.
4. **The narrator narrates the mechanics.** The casting sweep (NARRATOR lines in `Content/Run7VoiceManifest.ts`) says "Your chair: a witness.", "Your kettle: witness.", "Your mug: a memory."
5. **The items feel random.**
   - Most evidence is `object_memory` held by whatever furniture the scan found (lamp, mug, door latch, window glass, bed cover).
   - The baseline tell questions ("My lighter is empty", "Both my gloves are dry") hang on more furniture.
   - Nothing in the story explains why a sofa or a plant would know anything.

## The game in one sentence
Harlow died in this room tonight, four guests were here, and one of them killed him. Walk up to them, ask, show them what you've found, catch the lie and name the killer.

## Design rules (check every change against these)
1. **One voice at a time, and nobody is cut off.** A line that starts finishes, unless the case ends.
2. **Nothing explains the mechanics out loud.**
   - The narrator gives noir mood and the detective's own observations.
   - Wren is a partner who asks a question when you're stuck. She never gives instructions.
   - Spoken lines never use the words witness, memory, pinch, tap, button, notebook, palm, drag or token.
3. **Every item makes sense in the story.** Someone in the story touched it, and finding it answers something a suspect lied about. Your furniture is the stage, not a witness.
4. **One way to act: look and pinch once.** No drags, no holds and no palm menus.
5. **One row by the person, always the same three: Ask · Show · Accuse.** It's always in the same place and always behaves the same way.
6. **Catching a lie is the reward.** The suspect cracks, and the room shows the ghost of what really happened.
7. **The engine still decides everything.** Replays, reactions and headlines show only engine facts and admitted statements.
8. **Less.** A feature that doesn't serve find, ask and catch is taken out of the flow.

## The loop

### 1. Opening (under a minute)
- Harlow's voicemail plays over the room. The narrator then gets two lines of mood.
- The four guests are already standing in your room. The arrival dialogue already exists ("Good evening, Detective.").
- Setting:
  - crime-scene tape across the real doorway;
  - a chalk outline where Harlow fell, in the Study by the desk;
  - rain on the window.
- **One case file on the nearest table.** One pinch opens it, shows Harlow's photograph and "22:30", and the case begins.
- **What goes:**
  - the crate placement step and the two-file choice;
  - the casting sweep.
- The generated case moves to "Another case?" after the ending.

### 2. Find
- The items sit on your real surfaces, each in its place (see the case table).
- **Pinching an item:**
  - the narrator says one short observation in the first person ("Stone cold. Nobody made tea tonight.");
  - the item is now yours to show.

  Engine: `PinchObject` on the holder, as today.
- **If the player is stuck,** the Run 9 next-thing glow and Wren help. Nothing else glows.

### 3. Ask
- Walk up to someone and the row appears beside them.
- **Ask** opens in place into **at most four** natural questions for this person, plus **"Ask your own…"**, the existing typed free question answered live and checked by the engine.
- The answer comes in their voice, with the two-row subtitle below the face. Tells show on real lies only.
- **Baseline tell questions leave the list.**

### 4. Show
- **Show** opens in place into the items you've found, as small pictures. It's dimmed until you've found one.
- Pinch one to show it to the person you're facing. Engine: `Object` on their current statement with that evidence, or the existing present path, whichever the engine already uses for this.
- **If it breaks their statement:**
  - they crack, using the rattled or broken frame;
  - they say their next-layer line;
  - a 5-second ghost replay of the truth plays where it happened (`RoomscriptObjectReplay` on the illustrated ghost art).

  Examples: Vale was at the Study desk at 22:30, not in the Kitchen; Imran was in the Hall taking his note from Harlow's coat.
- **If it doesn't:** a short dismissive line, and the engine applies its usual cost.

### 5. Accuse
- **Accuse** sits in the same row: the person you're facing is the who.
- **"Where?"** The five places appear as small chalk-label cards in the row spot, and the matching place glows in the room while you hover.
- **"With what?"** The weapons appear as item pictures.
- **Seal:** one pinch on the wax seal. Engine: `Accuse` with who, where and what.
- **What follows:**
  - the accused reacts (the broken frame if right, smug if wrong);
  - you walk around the frozen murder;
  - the newspaper appears in your hands, with stats from the real play;
  - "Another case?" is offered.

## The hero case, remapped (HARLOW-1041)
**The truth doesn't change.** Vale strangled Harlow with the charger cable in the Study at 22:30 and staged it as heart failure. The other three lie about where they were to hide their own secrets.

**Change only where evidence lives and how it's found.** Keep the facts, statements, envelope and reducer rules. Move evidence holders. Add the missing physical items as virtual props in `Content/`. Keep `kind:"object_memory"` wherever that's what `PinchObject` reveals; only the presentation words change. The solver and fuzz tests must still prove the case solvable, and every content change goes in `decisions.md`.

- **Weapons for the accusation:** the cable, candlestick, letter knife, medicine bottle and marble bookend, each in its place. They are possible weapons in a murder room, which makes sense on its own. The engine's location facts for them stay.
- **Leaving the flow (the engine may keep the data if tests need it):**
  - the furniture memories: lamp, mug, door latch, window glass, clock;
  - the baseline tell lies;
  - Clue suggestions and refutation cards.

  Harlow being in the Study comes from the chalk outline and the recorder. If the solver needs `F_VICTIM_STUDY_*`, give it a sensible holder (the recorder, or the desk where he fell).
- **Timed beats:** keep only story moments. Pike reaching for the door, where the tape holds him, stays. So does the voicemail on the recorder. The blackout and UV beat goes. The letter is simply found.

## UI
- **The row:**
  - **Look:** three medium tokens, Ask · Show · Accuse, at chest height to the person's right (the `ActorArt` anchors), never over a face.
  - **When it appears:** when you're within 2 m and the person is within about 35° of your view centre for 0.3 s.
  - **When it hides:** only when you're beyond 2.6 m or more than 70° away for 1 s.
  - **While someone speaks:** the row hides, and it comes back when the line ends.
  - Same three tokens, same place, every time.
- **Opening a token:** Ask, Show and the two Accuse steps open in the row's own spot and close after the choice. Only one text layer is visible in any direction of view.
- **Subtitles:** the Run 9 two-row subtitle below the face.
- **Taken out of the flow:**
  - the chip bar and the verb bar;
  - the case-lid tokens;
  - the palm notebook and the evidence wallet;
  - memory cards and photo presenting;
  - the case board;
  - the evidence threads and web;
  - the zone plaques (place names show only during "Where?");
  - the objection pull;
  - UV dusting, the flashlight and the blackout;
  - the crate ceremony.
- **Stays:**
  - the Preview-only controls hint;
  - the next-thing glow (for being stuck);
  - the door tape;
  - the chalk outline;
  - the storm on the real window (atmosphere only).

## Audio
- **One speech channel.** Narrator, Wren, suspects and dialogue all go through one arbiter (extend `RoomscriptAudio`'s queue). Sound effects and music stay separate, and they duck under speech.
- **Priority:** the suspect answering you, then the narrator, then Wren.
- **Nothing interrupts a line in progress** except a case change or the ending.
- **Walking away mid-answer:** the line finishes and fades with distance.
- **No new question can start mid-answer:** the row is hidden while someone talks.
- **Filler:** play it only if the real answer isn't ready within about 1.2 s, and let it finish before the answer starts.
- **Wren:**
  - at most one line per 45 s;
  - only after 25 s stuck on the current step;
  - dropped if any other voice is queued or playing;
  - whispered from behind the player (spatial).
- **The script:** write every new or changed spoken line in `docs/SCRIPT_2026-11-08.md` before generating it.
  - **Narrator:** about 12 lines in all: 2 for the opening, one observation per item, 1–2 for the ending.
  - **Wren:** about 6 new lines that ask rather than tell ("What was she doing in a kitchen with a cold kettle?", "Somebody here is lying about where they were.").
  - **New suspect reactions** only where an item needs one that isn't banked yet.
  - Run the wording check from rule 2 over the whole script.
  - Generate with the cast voices in `Tooling/audio` (ElevenLabs). Subtitles are the fallback.

## Generated (dynamic) cases
- **Same rules:** the same row, the same one-voice audio and no casting sweep.
- **Evidence:** the compiler must give each evidence item a physical reason (who touched it, and what it shows). Where it can't, it uses placed props, not your furniture.
- **Priority:** below the hero case. If it isn't ready, it stays behind "Another case?" with a short known-issues note.

## Done when
1. **A first-time player can solve the hero case** using only item pinches and Ask, Show and Accuse, with no drags. Update the naive-player LEAF to prove it, and record the action count.
2. **Audio:** a full play logs zero overlapping voices and zero lines that ended early, apart from a case change or the ending. Add a LEAF that logs every speech start and end.
3. **The row:** scripted walking and turning jitter inside the hysteresis band never toggles the row. Add a LEAF for this too.
4. **The script:** every narrator and Wren line passes the wording check.
5. **Director captures pass the WEARER_UX checklist:**
   - the opening;
   - Vale at 1.4 m with the row;
   - the Show list;
   - a caught lie with its ghost replay;
   - Accuse at "Where?";
   - the frozen murder;
   - the newspaper.
6. **Tests:** vitest and the engine tests pass, the solver proves the remapped hero case solvable, and fuzz passes. LEAF scenarios for removed features are retired, not left failing.
7. **Size:** the exported lens size is measured and stays under the 25 MB limit.

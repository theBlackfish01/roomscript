# Illustrated cast, real props (Nov 7): paper theatre done properly

**Primary art spec from Run 9 onward.** It replaces the Meshy 3D plan in [ART_3D_2026-11-06.md](ART_3D_2026-11-06.md), which is now historical. [The wearer UX spec](WEARER_UX_2026-11-06.md) still governs interaction, staging and the wow moments. The reasons are in [the Run 8 review](REVIEW_2026-11-06-run8.md).

## Decision (Wadood, Nov 7, after midnight)

**Keep the characters 2D, but replace the code-drawn line art with high-quality, *stylised* illustrations made by an image model.** Make the props real 3D with Snap's own generator, and the paper objects real textured objects.

- **The image model:** OpenAI's image models are the main route. The key is `OPENAI_API_KEY` in `Tooling/.env`, provided by Wadood and tested Nov 7. Open models are the fallback.
- **Stylised, not photorealistic** (Wadood).
- **Code stays for the geometric work:** props, paper, the crate, rain, lightning, threads, UI. Faces are where agent-written paths and Three.js renders hit their ceiling; the current cards are Three.js renders.

**Why:**
- **The look, not the dimension, was the problem.** The current cards are SVG paths written by an agent, which is why they look like doodles. An image model draws faces, hands and clothing properly.
- **Reading faces is the game** (L.A. Noire). Illustrated expressions keep that. Generated 3D rigs have no facial animation.
- **Low risk.** The card system already handles 5 poses per suspect, billboarding, the talking mouth, tells, the stress rim and ghosts. Mostly the textures change.
- **Cheap, with a free fallback.**
  - OpenAI images cost cents each.
  - The fallback models are open: Qwen-Image and Qwen-Image-Edit (Apache 2.0), FLUX.1-schnell (Apache 2.0), SDXL (OpenRAIL++-M).
  - Background removal is open: BiRefNet or rembg (MIT).
  - The fallback runs on Hugging Face Spaces, or locally on Wadood's RTX 4060 (8 GB).
- **It doesn't grow the lens,** which is already near its 25 MB limit.
- **It's the original concept:** "paper theatre and forensic light". Illustrated standees in your real room are that idea done properly.

**Accepted trade-off:** characters stay standees that turn to face you. The frozen murder can be walked around, but the figures turn with you, and walking is a paper-theatre glide, not a stride.

## The look: illustrated people, solid objects, light evidence

| Illustrated (2D, high quality) | Solid (real textured 3D) | Light (glowing lines, existing) |
|---|---|---|
| The four suspects and Harlow | Virtual props (candlestick, letter knife, bookend, and any fixture the Preview room lacks) | Evidence threads, convergence, the web |
| Their expressions and tell frames | The evidence crate (wood and brass, opening lid) | Chalk circles, the outline, crosses |
| | The case folders, the newspaper, memory photographs, the board, the wax seal (paper and print textures) | Ghosts: the same illustrations with a ghost material |
| | | Glyph tokens, subtitles, spotlight, rings, stamps, rain and lightning, the UV torch |

**Style: stylised graphic-novel noir.** It was chosen Nov 7 from three test renders, after Wadood asked for "a more stylised option instead of photorealistic".

**The reference image** is [docs/art/2026-11-07-vale-style-reference.png](art/2026-11-07-vale-style-reference.png): `gpt-image-2`, quality medium, 1024×1536, plain light-grey background. Every character must match it: the clean ink outline, flat colour with soft cel shading, slightly elongated elegant proportions, a light warm palette, and a subtle rim light in the accent colour.

The style block used for it (reuse it word for word, after the character's brief):

> Style: stylized, not photorealistic: modern graphic-novel noir character art, like concept art for an animated mystery film. Bold simplified shapes, slightly elongated elegant proportions, clean confident ink outlines, flat colour blocks with soft cel shading, light warm palette, subtle [accent] rim light. Plain flat light-grey background, no props, no text.

**Set aside:**
- the luminous-engraving version (too close to realistic);
- a `gpt-image-1.5` transparent-background version (a generic cartoon look).

Keep palettes light, with one accent per character. Wadood asked for slight additive-display adjustments only.

### Frames per suspect

These map onto the existing `renderPose` states (neutral, listening, rattled, broken, smug), with two additions:

| Frame | Used when |
|---|---|
| neutral | default |
| listening (speaking) | while their voice plays |
| speaking, mouth open | swapped with "listening" by speech amplitude, giving a simple lip flap; replaces the jaw warp if it reads better |
| rattled | layer above 0, or stress ≥ 0.3 |
| broken | layer ≥ 2, or composure gone |
| smug | for 1.8 s after a wrong objection |
| **tell** (new) | their tell fires (the table above), together with the existing gaze line |

**Vale also needs a tableau frame:** both hands holding the cable taut. Harlow needs standing, staggering and fallen.

**Ghosts** use the same frames with a ghost material: tint, partial alpha, a soft dissolve. This retires the 8 separate ghost claim/truth PNGs.

**Identity must hold across frames.** Make the variants with an edit model from the chosen base image (Qwen-Image-Edit), or with low-strength inpainting of the face and hands only. Check by looking at a contact sheet. The same hair, clothing and proportions must read as the same person.

## Pipeline

1. **Where it runs:**
   - **OpenAI (main route):** `OPENAI_API_KEY` in `Tooling/.env`. Models seen on the key Nov 7: `gpt-image-2`, `gpt-image-1.5`, `gpt-image-1-mini`, and newer `gpt-image-2.5-*` and `2.6-*` variants.
     - Check docs.openai.com for current parameters before the first batch.
     - **`gpt-image-2` rejects `background: "transparent"`.** Either generate on a plain flat background and cut out (step 4), or use `gpt-image-1.5` with a transparent background if its quality is comparable. The Nov 7 samples decide.
     - **Requests can take more than 5 minutes.** Node's `fetch` gives up after 300 s, so use `node:https` with no timeout. Run requests in the background, at most about 3 at a time.
     - **Variants:** use the edits endpoint with the chosen base image as a reference input, with high input fidelity if offered. This keeps identity across frames.
     - **Ledger:** `Tooling/results/openai-images-ledger.json` (model, size, quality, usage, file, purpose).
     - **Budget:** up to **$25** of image generation this run. Rule 18 applies; switch to the fallback on a quota error.
     - **Licensing:** OpenAI's terms assign outputs to the user. Record "generated with OpenAI <model>" in `NOTICE`.
   - **Fallback: Hugging Face Spaces, if `HF_TOKEN` is in `Tooling/.env`:** call them from a Node script with `@gradio/client` (MIT), or from Python with `gradio_client`. Use `mcp-tools/Qwen-Image` or `Qwen-Image-Fast` and `evalstate/flux1_schnell` for generation, `Qwen-Image-Edit` Spaces for variants, and `not-lain/background-removal` (BiRefNet) for cutouts.
     - These Spaces run on ZeroGPU, which has a daily quota.
     - On a quota error, switch to the local route (rule 18).
   - **Fallback: locally on the RTX 4060 (8 GB):**
     - Make a Python venv in `Tooling/art2d/.venv` (gitignored), with torch (CUDA) and diffusers.
     - Use SDXL, plus SDXL inpainting for expressions, or FLUX.1-schnell as GGUF Q4 with offloading.
     - **RAM is 16 GB, shared with Lens Studio.** Run generation in batches, never while a LEAF run or a Chromium render is going, and free the model afterwards.
   - **Licences for the open route:** ship only art from Apache, MIT or OpenRAIL-licensed weights (Qwen-Image or -Edit, FLUX.1-schnell, SDXL). Don't ship output from FLUX.1 [dev]-family weights (dev, Krea, Kontext dev), which have a non-commercial licence. Record the model, its licence, prompt and seed in `NOTICE` and `Tooling/art2d/ledger.json`.
2. **Base image:**
   - The prompt is the brief, then: full body, standing facing the viewer, head to shoes visible, then the style block above with the character's accent.
   - Use 1024×1536 at quality medium; use high for the final base if medium is soft.
   - **Lock the style across the cast:** also pass the Vale reference image as an input image (edits endpoint): "a new character in exactly the same art style as the reference image".
   - Make 2–4 candidates per character, look at them next to the Vale reference, pick one, and record the choice.
3. **Variants:** the frames above, made with the edits endpoint from the chosen base, keeping everything but the expression, hands or pose identical. Make a contact sheet per character.
4. **Cutout:** use BiRefNet or rembg (local, CPU is fine), then clean the edges (erode about 1 px, defringe the grey). Optionally add a 1–2 px paper-white outline for the paper-theatre feel.
5. **Normalise:**
   - Use the same canvas per character, with the feet on a common baseline and real-world height (about 170 cm) mapped consistently.
   - Palette-quantise with `sharp` (libimagequant). Illustrations usually shrink 60–70%.
6. **Anchors:** for each character and frame, record normalised points in `Assets/Scripts/Content/ActorArt.ts`:
   - the face box;
   - the eye point;
   - the mouth box;
   - the tell-prop point;
   - the right hand (where the cable attaches in the tableau);
   - the feet.

   Measure them by looking at the images, or with a face detector such as MediaPipe (Apache 2.0). The actor reads these instead of the hard-coded card heights; [the review](REVIEW_2026-11-06-run8.md) lists each one: +145 face avoidance, +130 facing, +118 threads, +115 spotlight, token and subtitle heights, tell prop positions, the gaze start.
7. **Import:** replace the textures behind the existing actor. Keep the old cards available behind a `characterArt` switch (`"illustrated"` or `"line"`) until Wadood signs off.
8. **Present:**
   - **Contact shadow:** a soft contact shadow, or a thin light line at the feet in the additive look, so standees feel placed on the floor.
   - **Motion:** a gentle breathing sway (a vertex or shader offset), a head bob on speech, and a small bob-and-lean glide when they move.
   - **Shader:** keep the stress rim (ivory → amber → coral). Remove the "darken detail beyond 2 m" effect if it hurts the new art.

## Props and paper objects

**Props:** use CLAD `build-mesh` (SPECS text- or image-to-3D through the signed-in Lens Studio). It needs no key, gives static, textured meshes, and counts as CLAD usage.
- Gate each with `analyze_glb.js` (normals, scale, facing). Optimise with `specs-optimize-lens-mesh` to at most about 3k triangles and 256–512² textures.
- **Which props:** the virtual stand-ins, plus any fixture the Preview Evening Room has no real counterpart for. A fixture that matches real furniture gets a highlight, not a mesh.
- **Retire** the wireframe props.

**The crate:** make two meshes (body and lid) so the lid can open: dark wood, brass corners, a stencilled case number.

**Paper objects:** use canvas textures from `Tooling/art` (headless Chromium). Type and layout are code, so they come out sharp. Text comes from the engine at runtime, or is re-rendered per state.
- Manila folders: a coral "THE HARLOW TRUST" stamp and a string tie. The blank Dynamic folder has a brass magnifier.
- The newspaper: opaque newsprint, held at about 60 cm.
- Memory photographs: photo paper with a white border.
- The board: cork with brass pins.
- The wax seal: coral wax.

## Vale first (about 45–60 minutes)

The style is already chosen; this step proves it in the room.

1. Start from the reference image (or a fresh candidate if it's better). Make her neutral and speaking frames, cut them out, normalise, import, and record anchors.
2. Capture her and the current card at the same spot:
   - conversation framing at 1.4 m;
   - full figure at 3 m;
   - a 45° view.
3. **Check that:**
   - the face reads sharp at 1.4 m. If a single full-body texture is soft, split it into a body layer and a high-resolution head layer; expressions then change only the head;
   - she sits on the floor with a contact shadow;
   - she reads against the subtitle and tokens.
4. Tune the presentation (outline, rim, shadow, scale), record it in `decisions.md` with the captures, then produce the whole cast to match.

If the illustrated art somehow doesn't beat the card in the room, keep the card faces, record why, and continue. **Art never stops the run.**

## Budgets

**Size:** the published Spectacles lens limit is 25 MB, and the current export is estimated at 18–21 MB but never measured. Measure a real export first, then make room:
- drop unreferenced WAVs and old art;
- minify `VoiceManifest.ts`;
- check whether `Echopark.hdr` is needed;
- drop the 8 ghost PNGs once ghosts use the material.

**Per asset:**
- **Characters:** at most about 1.3 MB of palette-quantised PNG per suspect, and at most 0.5 MB for Harlow. 512×1024 per frame, or a 512×1024 body plus a 512² head.
- **Props:** at most about 3k triangles, 256–512² textures, about 0.2 MB each.

**Totals:** under 100 draw calls.

**Money:** at most $25 of OpenAI image generation this run, with no other paid services. Rule 18 applies to the image budget and the Space quotas.

## Visual checklist additions

These add to the WEARER_UX checklist.

9. Faces are sharp and readable at 1.4 m. Each character is the same person across all frames, stands on the floor with a contact shadow, and is never inside furniture.
10. Palettes stay light, with the accent visible. No dark-on-dark areas carry the read.
11. Props and paper objects are textured solids, not wireframes. The newspaper and folders are opaque.
12. Ghosts are visibly not the living characters.
13. Text never overlaps text. One text layer per direction of view.

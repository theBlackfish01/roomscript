# Real 3D art pass (Nov 6): solid people, light evidence

> **Superseded on Nov 7 by [ART_2D_2026-11-07.md](ART_2D_2026-11-07.md)** (Wadood's decision): stylised 2D illustrations from an image model, not Meshy 3D. This file is kept for the record. Its prop, paper-object and budget thinking carried over.

**Was the primary art spec for Run 9.** It works alongside [the wearer UX spec](WEARER_UX_2026-11-06.md), which still governs interaction, staging and the wow moments. The reasons are in [the Run 8 review](REVIEW_2026-11-06-run8.md).

## Decision (Wadood asked for it; made Nov 6 evening)

**Replace the flat character cards and wireframe props with real 3D assets.** The characters become rigged, animated, stylised 3D people, made with Meshy's API. Props and paper objects become textured 3D objects.

- **Why:** the Run 8 captures show that the interaction design works. The look, however, reads as a prototype:
  - the suspects are neon doodles on flat cards, which turn edge-on when you walk around them;
  - the props are thin wireframes, and the desk lamp reads as a martini glass;
  - the "walk around the frozen murder" moment is a set of cutouts.
  
  Judges rate Design highest, and the video is recorded in Preview, where real 3D reads far better than line art.
- **Why not just better line art:** line art can't be hand-drawn by an agent at quality. Two runs of procedural line art have hit that ceiling.
- **Why Meshy:** it is the only service that does every step from a terminal: image or text to 3D, auto-rigging, a library of more than 600 animations, and text-to-motion for custom gestures. The free plan has no API, so Wadood buys Premium; see "Owner actions".
- **Kept as a fallback:** the current card renderer stays behind a `characterStyle` switch (`"3d"` or `"cards"`) until Wadood's Saturday review.

## The look: solid people, light evidence

The room and the people in it are **solid**. What the detective sees in their mind is **light**.

| Solid: real textured 3D | Light: glowing lines (existing style) |
|---|---|
| The four suspects; Harlow's body where the story shows it | Evidence threads, the convergence and the web |
| Virtual props (candlestick, letter knife, bookend, mug, recorder, clock, kettle, bottle, cable) | Chalk zone circles, the chalk outline, chalk crosses |
| The evidence crate (wood and brass) and the two case folders (paper, stamp, string) | Ghosts: replays, the lightning ghost, the reconstruction. These reuse the same 3D characters with a ghost material |
| The newspaper (opaque paper in your hands), memory photographs (photo paper), the wax seal | Glyph tokens, subtitles, the spotlight beam, rings, stamps, the UV torch |
| The case board (cork or wood, pinned paper) | Rain and lightning; the crime-scene tape can be either, so pick whichever reads better |

**Additive display: slight adjustments only (Wadood).**
- Light-coloured clothing with one accent colour each.
- An unlit material with baked shading plus a rim light in the accent colour.
- Pure black never carries the read.
- Don't redesign the look for the device. Preview is the demo.

### Animations: game behaviour → clip

Use clips from Meshy's library where they exist, and text-to-motion where they don't. Request every clip from the **same rig task** as its character.

| Game moment | Clip |
|---|---|
| Waiting in the room | 2 idle variants, desynchronised per suspect |
| Player approaches or asks | Listening |
| Speaking (voice playing) | 2 talk variants (hands open, right hand) and a speech-amplitude head bob on the head bone. There are no blendshapes, so don't attempt lip-sync |
| Their tell fires | The per-suspect tell clip above, plus a head look-at toward the tell object, clamped to about ±60° yaw and ±30° pitch, in `LateUpdateEvent` |
| Objection sustained against them | A small flinch or half-step back |
| Composure gone ("lawyers up") | Arms crossed, turned slightly away |
| You face them while choosing the accused | Freeze and stare back |
| Accused correctly / wrongly | Head lowers / a scoff |
| Pike's escape to the door; suspects stepping out at the ending | Walk in place; a script moves the root. Check for root motion first |
| Frozen murder at 22:30 | Vale holding the cable taut and Harlow staggering back, each a single held frame (`playClipAt`, then `pauseClip`) |
| Replays and the lightning ghost | The same clips, with the ghost material |

**Blending:** there is no crossfade call. Ramp `AnimationClip.weight` in a script over about 0.25 s.

**Performance:** pause the AnimationPlayers of suspects who aren't on screen or in focus.

### Materials

- **`RoomscriptCharacter`:**
  - unlit;
  - base colour texture × exposure, with soft baked shading from the texture;
  - fresnel rim in the accent colour;
  - a small lift so dark areas still read.
  - Tune it in Preview against the checklist. Most of the read should come from the texture, not from glow.
- **`RoomscriptGhost`:**
  - the same mesh with fresnel only;
  - accent or ghost cyan, partly transparent;
  - optional subtle scanlines (the Material Library "Hologram" graph is a starting point).

## Props and paper objects

**Props: Snap's tools first.** Try the CLAD `build-mesh` skill (SPECS text or image to 3D through the signed-in Lens Studio; FAST3D is the quick option). It needs no key, gives static meshes, and counts as CLAD usage. Fall back to Meshy text-to-3D for any prop that comes out poor.

**Which props need a mesh:**
- **Virtual stand-ins:** the candlestick, letter knife and bookend need a mesh.
- **Fixtures:** where the Preview Evening Room has no real counterpart (check each: kettle, mug, recorder, clock, medicine bottle, lamp), they need a mesh as well. A fixture that matches real furniture gets a highlight only, no mesh.
- **On device:** fixtures bind to the player's real objects, and only the substitutes are virtual.

**Hero objects:**
- **The crate:** an evidence box in dark wood and brass, with the case number stencilled on and a working lid.
- **The two folders:** a manila Preset folder with "THE HARLOW TRUST" stamped in coral and a wax-sealed string tie; the Dynamic folder blank, with a brass magnifier on top.
- **The newspaper:** opaque newsprint with a printed layout, held at about 60 cm.
- **Memory photographs:** photo paper with a white border.
- **The board:** cork with brass pins.
- **The wax seal:** coral wax.

**Paper and print textures:** render them with the existing `Tooling/art` pipeline (headless Chromium canvas). Type and layout are code, so they come out sharp. Keep all text from the engine; the textures are templates with text drawn at runtime, or regenerated per state.

## Pipeline

1. **Reference.** For each character, make a full-body, front-facing A-pose reference in colour from the brief. Use Meshy text-to-image or image-to-image if available (check the docs at run start). Otherwise go straight to text-to-3D with the brief.
   - Make 2–4 variants and pick by looking at them.
   - Don't feed the line-art portraits straight into image-to-3D: line art gives lumpy faces.
2. **Image or text to 3D:** use `ai_model` latest, `pose_mode: "a-pose"`, `should_remesh: true`, `target_polycount` about 12000, and GLB plus FBX output.
   - Set the polycount here, because a skinned mesh can't be decimated later.
3. **Rig:** `POST /openapi/v1/rigging` with `height_meters`.
4. **Animations:** library clips (up to 10 per merged request) plus text-to-motion for tells and the tableau poses. Apply each to the same rig.
5. **Gate every file** with CLAD's `analyze_glb.js`:
   - normals present (the Run 8 GLB props had no normals and never rendered);
   - about 170 cm tall;
   - facing +Z, then corrected to Lens Studio's −Z;
   - at most 100 joints.
   
   If normals are missing, import the FBX instead; its importer can regenerate normals for skinned meshes.
6. **Import:**
   - Turn the **Legacy Importer** off, or AnimationPlayer won't work.
   - GLBs come in at 100× scale, and the root sometimes gets an extra −90° X rotation. Fix the size when importing (`normalize_glb.js`). Scale a wrapper object, never the prefab itself. Don't apply both the metres-to-centimetres conversion and the 100× fix.
   - Add the extra animation files as clips; they bind to bones by name.
7. **Wire and stage:**
   - **First add the actor API:** anchors (head, eyes, chest, right hand, feet) and clip states, used by both the card and the 3D actor. Route every hard-coded card height and tell position through it; [the review](REVIEW_2026-11-06-run8.md) lists each one. Then the `characterStyle` switch is just a different actor behind the same API.
   - Walking turns the body along its path; no yaw billboard on 3D characters.
   - The cable in the tableau attaches to the right-hand bone.
   - suspects stand on free floor at the existing conversation spots, at 1.3–1.5 m for conversation framing;
   - the head look-at runs after animation;
   - each suspect has a voice-amplitude head bob.
8. **Record and log:**
   - Keep a ledger in `Tooling/results/meshy-ledger.json`: task id, type, credits, time and output file.
   - Source files go to `Tooling/art3d/` (gitignored if large); shipped assets go to `Assets/Art/Characters/` and `Assets/Art/Props/`.
   - Record provenance in `NOTICE`, `devpost/clad-log.md` and the Devpost text, honestly. For example: "Characters generated, rigged and animated with Meshy from our briefs; props with Lens Studio's 3D generation."

## Spike first: Vale end to end (go/no-go within about 2 hours)

Build Vale completely before anyone else: reference, model, rig, idle, talk, one tell, material, staging, look-at.

**Captures (rule 19):**
- conversation framing at 1.4 m;
- full figure at 3 m;
- a side view at 90°;
- a side-by-side with the current card at the same spot.

**Go if all of these hold:**
1. She reads as a believable, appealing person at both distances. The face isn't lumpy or uncanny, and the hands are acceptable at 1.4 m.
2. Idle, talk and the tell play correctly, and the head look-at works.
3. Normals, scale and facing are right, and she stands on the floor, not in furniture.
4. Frame time is measured and isn't catastrophically worse; record the number.
5. She is clearly better than the card in the side-by-side.

**No-go handling:**
- Try up to **three** generation attempts (a different reference, model setting or polycount) before calling no-go.
- On no-go: keep the cards, spend the art time on better card faces and the prop and paper pass, and record the reason in `decisions.md`.
- **Never stop the run over art quality.**

**On go:** do Qadir, Okafor, Pike and Harlow, then wire the full animation map.

## Budgets

**Size is the tight one.** The current lens is estimated at 18–21 MB but has never been measured by export, and the published Spectacles limit is 25 MB. So:
1. **Measure a real export first** (Run 9 step 0).
2. **Make room:**
   - drop unreferenced WAVs and old actor art;
   - minify `VoiceManifest.ts` (2.1 MB of pretty-printed alignment);
   - check whether `Echopark.hdr` (1.4 MB) is needed;
   - once 3D is a go, drop four of the five pose PNGs per suspect, keeping one portrait for the board and newspaper (about 2.9 MB).
3. **Set the character budget from the measured headroom.**

**Per asset:**
- **Characters:** about 1 MB each.
  - 8–10k triangles, with `target_polycount` set at generation;
  - one mesh and one material;
  - a compressed 512² base colour texture;
  - at most 100 joints with 4 influences per vertex;
  - only the clips the animation map needs, sampled at 30 fps with curve optimisation on.
- **Props:** at most about 3k triangles and 256–512² textures, about 0.2 MB each.

**Totals:**
- about 50k triangles on screen in total;
- under 100 draw calls.

**Lens size and money:**
- If the export is still over 25 MB, the Preview demo still works; it doesn't block development (rule 18). Record it under Needs Wadood.
- Meshy credits: Premium has 3,000. Characters take about 120 each, so about 600 for five, or about 900 with retries; props about 30 each.
- Rule 18 applies: running out of credits stops generation only. Use the cards and props already made.

## Visual checklist additions

These add to the WEARER_UX checklist.

9. Characters are solid 3D, stand on the floor, face the player when spoken to, and never intersect furniture.
10. Character colours stay light, with the accent rim visible. No dark-on-dark costume areas carry the read.
11. Props and paper objects are textured solids, not wireframes. The newspaper and folders are opaque.
12. Ghosts and replays are visibly *not* the living characters: ghost material, cyan or coral, partly transparent.
13. Text never overlaps other text, whether subtitles, titles or paper print. One text layer per direction of view.
